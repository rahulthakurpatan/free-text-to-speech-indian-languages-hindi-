AI Voiceover Subtitles: Create Accurate SRT Captions

A practical workflow to turn your final synthetic narration into readable, properly timed captions—without pretending script-only timestamps are accurate.

17 November 2026·Vāṇī AI Editorial

Quick answer

Finish and export your AI voiceover first. Transcribe or align that exact recording, correct the timestamps while listening, export a UTF-8 SRT file, and test it against the final video. A text-to-speech script alone does not guarantee synchronized captions.

Key takeaways

  • Final audio—not estimated reading speed—determines caption timing.
  • Validate timestamps and spelling, especially after editing or stitching clips.
  • SRT uses numbered cues and comma-separated milliseconds.
  • Check Unicode and language-specific terms for Hindi and regional languages.

Why AI voiceovers still need caption alignment

Generating narration from a script does not automatically produce a correctly timed subtitle file. Speech models can pause, rephrase, pronounce numerals differently, or create different clip durations between generations. If you estimate timestamps from the number of words in the script, subtitles may drift from the audio.

The reliable workflow is to finish the audio first, then align the actual spoken recording to captions. That applies to Hindi, English and other language voiceovers.

A six-step workflow for SRT subtitles

  1. Prepare a spoken-word script. Remove stage directions that will not be read aloud. Expand ambiguous numerals and abbreviations.
  2. Generate the final narration. Use your chosen TTS voice, listen to the entire file and regenerate any clipped or mispronounced segment.
  3. Export one continuous audio track. If you produced chapters separately, assemble them in the correct order, including intended silence.
  4. Transcribe or align the finished audio. Use an editor with automatic captions or an audio-transcription workflow that supports timestamps. Treat generated times as drafts, not ground truth.
  5. Review each subtitle against playback. Correct wording, spelling, speaker changes and start/end times, especially near edits.
  6. Export SRT and test it in your video editor. Play the beginning, middle and end; ensure there is no progressive drift.

SRT file format: a working example

SubRip (.srt) captions contain a sequential cue number, a start/end time, caption text and a blank line. Milliseconds use a comma, not a decimal point.

1
00:00:00,500 --> 00:00:03,100
Welcome to our lesson.

2
00:00:03,400 --> 00:00:06,700
Today we will learn how sound travels.

This is an illustrative sample, not timings measured from a recording. In a real SRT, adjust each cue to the actual audio.

Caption timing and readability checks

  • Begin a caption close to when its first word is spoken and end it after the final word, without obscuring the next cue.
  • Prefer natural phrase boundaries; do not split a person's name or tightly connected phrase merely to fit a character count.
  • Keep lines comfortably readable on a phone and avoid excessive text on screen.
  • When the narration has a meaningful pause, allow the caption to clear rather than displaying text too early.
  • Inspect fast speech, numbers, acronyms and multilingual code-switching manually.

There is no universal characters-per-second rule for every language or platform. Use your editor's accessibility recommendations and test on the intended screen size.

Hindi and regional-language caption tips

Use the language's correct writing system where practical. For Hindi, compare the final audio against Devanagari text, not only Romanized notes. Check proper nouns, place names, English technical words, honorifics and digits carefully. A speaker may say a number in words while a draft caption shows digits; choose a consistent representation that preserves meaning.

For bilingual narration, ensure the subtitle editor exports UTF-8 text. Open the final SRT in a plain-text editor and confirm that non-Latin characters display correctly before uploading.

Fix five common caption problems

Captions gradually drift

Re-align against the exported final audio, not the original unedited chunks. Check whether the editor changed playback speed or inserted silence.

Captions are too early

Shift the cue start to the actual onset of speech; do not rely on script reading-speed estimates.

Words are missing

Compare with the audio. Transcription models can omit repeated words or filler phrases.

Hindi letters look broken

Save as UTF-8 and verify the player supports the chosen subtitle font.

SRT is rejected

Confirm numbering, the HH:MM:SS,mmm --> HH:MM:SS,mmm format, positive durations and blank lines between cues.

Frequently asked questions

Can I create SRT directly from a TTS script?

You can draft the words, but accurate timestamps require alignment to the finished audio. Script-only timing is approximate.

Does Vāṇī AI automatically generate subtitles?

Vāṇī AI is a text-to-voice studio. This article describes an external captioning workflow; it does not claim an integrated subtitle export feature.

Should I add subtitles before or after editing the voiceover?

After the narration is finalized. If you later cut or rearrange audio, re-check the caption timing.
