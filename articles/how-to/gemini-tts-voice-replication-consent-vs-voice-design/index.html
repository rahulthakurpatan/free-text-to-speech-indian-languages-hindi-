Gemini TTS Voice Replication vs Voice Design: Consent & Setup

Learn what is—and is not—a voice clone, how Google's consent recording rules work, and when a designed or prebuilt voice is the better fit.

Quick answer

Voice replication attempts to create a synthetic voice based on recordings of a particular adult speaker and requires that person's real consent. Voice design creates a new vocal persona from a description without copying a specific person's identity. Prebuilt voice selection chooses an existing model voice, as in Vāṇī's current voice selector. These are three different workflows. Do not assume a Gemini API key automatically enables all three in every region or product.

What does Gemini voice replication require?

Google documents two recordings of the same adult speaker for voice replication: a clean reference recording and a separate recording of that speaker reading the prescribed consent statement. Its current guide specifies WAV, mono, 24 kHz, 16-bit little-endian linear PCM for both. The reference is 60 seconds of clean natural speech; longer examples can improve results according to Google.

The consent recording must use the exact statement required for the selected language. Do not substitute a synthetic recording, a colleague reading for the speaker, a celebrity clip, or a paraphrased statement. Follow the current official statement and language list rather than copying an outdated quote from a tutorial.

Google's developer forum also explicitly states that synthetic reference and consent audio are not supported for replication. Read the official response in context.

Prepare usable recordings

  1. Confirm that the speaker is an adult and has knowingly agreed to the intended use.
  2. Record the reference in a quiet room, without music, echo or other speakers.
  3. Keep the microphone position and gain steady; avoid clipping.
  4. Record the language-specific consent statement separately, exactly as specified.
  5. Export both files in the required PCM WAV format and check their properties before upload.
  6. Store recordings with restricted access; do not post consent files or API keys publicly.
  7. Check model, region and account eligibility before starting a client project.

Audio conversion alone cannot repair a distorted recording. Re-record bad source audio rather than claiming an editor can reconstruct lost speech details.

Check the WAV format before uploading

Common file extensions are misleading: a file named sample.wav can still contain unsupported encoding. A typical local command to inspect a file is:

ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name,sample_rate,channels,bits_per_sample -of default=noprint_wrappers=1 reference.wav

For a recording you own and are authorized to process, an example conversion using FFmpeg is:

ffmpeg -i input.wav -ac 1 -ar 24000 -c:a pcm_s16le reference.wav

This is a format-conversion example, not a tested end-to-end Gemini replication call. Inspect the output and listen to it before uploading. Never fabricate consent by editing another person's speech.

When is voice design a better choice?

If your aim is a friendly, confident, mature narrator for a fictional channel—not the exact identity of a real person—voice design is usually the more appropriate concept. Describe vocal qualities rather than naming a celebrity or impersonating a private individual. Review availability and terms in the official voice design guide; it is not equivalent to a basic prebuilt-voice dropdown.

For creators who simply need a usable Hindi, Tamil or English narration today, a prebuilt voice may be the simplest route. Compare short passages using the documented Gemini voice names before committing to a larger workflow.

Why the same API key may not unlock replication

Gemini TTS products and endpoints have different features, availability and authentication requirements. Google Cloud voice replication documentation describes a specific Voices API and eligibility conditions. The normal Gemini text-to-speech generation route is not automatically the same thing. Check the current model list, supported region, API version and billing/eligibility requirements for your exact account. A 403 or 404 does not by itself prove your consent recording is wrong.

Do not promise clients that a particular voice can be cloned until you have confirmed feature access and completed an authorized trial. Voice replication can be restricted even when ordinary TTS works.

Safety, rights and disclosure checklist

  • Obtain informed, documented consent from the actual speaker before replicating their voice.
  • Use the required real consent recording; never synthesize or impersonate consent.
  • Define the permitted uses, duration and revocation process with the speaker.
  • Review the applicable Google terms, local law and distribution-platform rules.
  • Disclose synthetic or replicated speech when required, particularly for advertising or identity-sensitive uses.
  • Restrict access to source recordings and delete them when no longer needed under your retention policy.
  • Do not use voice replication for deception, fraud or unauthorized impersonation.

This is practical risk guidance, not legal advice. Rules vary by location and use case.

FAQ

Can I clone a celebrity voice with Gemini?

Do not assume public availability of a recording grants permission. Replication requires real speaker consent and applicable rights. Use an authorized or designed voice instead.

Can a generated AI voice be the reference?

Google's voice replication guidance requires recordings from a real adult speaker and does not support machine-generated reference or consent recordings for that process.

Does selecting Kore or Puck clone someone?

No. Selecting a documented prebuilt voice is not the same as replicating a specific human speaker.

Can Vāṇī Studio clone my voice?

No. Its current BYOK language studios offer supported prebuilt voice selection and generation, not voice replication enrollment.
