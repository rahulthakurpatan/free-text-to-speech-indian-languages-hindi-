Hindi TTS Numbers, Dates & Rupees: Fix Spoken Pronunciation

A practical script-normalization workflow for creators making Hindi or Hinglish voiceovers.

17 November 2026·Vāṇī AI Editorial·How-to guide

Quick answer

When Hindi TTS reads a number, date or currency incorrectly, replace the ambiguous digits or symbols with the exact words you want spoken. Use a different convention for amounts versus phone numbers and OTPs, then listen to a short generated sample before publishing.

Key takeaways

  • Decide what every number means before converting it.
  • Expand prices and percentages into natural spoken words.
  • Preserve leading zeroes and digit order in identifiers.
  • Test Hinglish pronunciation with the intended voice.
  • Review audio manually; no rewrite guarantees perfect output.

Why Hindi TTS reads numbers incorrectly

A string of digits is ambiguous. In a Hindi voiceover, 2026 might be a year, an ID, or four separate digits. ₹1,500 should usually sound like an amount, while a phone number must be read digit by digit. A speech engine cannot always infer the creator's intention. This is a text normalization problem: rewrite ambiguous symbols as the exact words you want the audience to hear.

Public creator discussions describe inconsistent number and language switching in multilingual TTS. That is evidence of a real user problem, not verified search-volume data or a guarantee that every Gemini voice behaves the same way.

A six-step number-normalization workflow

  1. Mark every ambiguous token. Search your draft for digits, currency signs, percentages, colons, slashes, decimals, IDs and acronyms.
  2. Label its meaning. Decide whether each token is a quantity, year, date, phone number, OTP, account identifier, measurement or address.
  3. Expand to spoken words. Write exactly what a narrator should say, using Hindi script when appropriate.
  4. Keep identifiers intact. A code such as 0042 is not forty-two; preserve leading zeros and read digits separately.
  5. Generate a short sample. Include a date, amount, proper name and acronym in a 15–30-second test.
  6. Listen and correct. Fix errors in the script, rerun only the affected segment and check the final audio before publishing.

For long recordings, keep a consistent spelling glossary across chunks; see our Gemini TTS long-text chunking guide.

Hinglish: prevent unexpected language switching

Mixed Hindi-English narration adds a second decision: which language should speak each token? A sentence such as “Your order is ₹2,499” can shift into an English number reading. If the audience expects Hindi, rewrite the entire amount as दो हज़ार चार सौ निन्यानवे रुपये; if the audience expects English, spell it out in English. Do not randomly switch conventions inside the same lesson.

For names and borrowed terms, keep a small pronunciation sheet: original spelling, preferred spoken spelling, language, and approved audio sample. If a proper noun is critical, manually audition it rather than assuming a transcription service will detect subtle pronunciation errors.

Special cases: decimals, phone numbers and years

Decimals and measurements

3.5 kg can be written तीन दशमलव पाँच किलोग्राम. For a classroom audience, “साढ़े तीन किलो” may be more natural; use the expression that preserves the intended precision.

Phone numbers and one-time codes

Read digit by digit, retain zeroes, and use commas or separate clauses to help pacing. Do not publish actual private phone numbers, OTPs or account identifiers in example scripts.

Years

Specify the spoken form. 2026 as a year is different from an item code or a quantity. For historical or documentary narration, keep a consistent year-reading style.

Pre-publish listening checklist

  • All amounts, percentages and units are spoken correctly.
  • Dates cannot be mistaken for another regional format.
  • Phone numbers and codes preserve every digit and leading zero.
  • Hindi and English switches match the intended audience.
  • Names, abbreviations and local place names are intelligible.
  • Output audio matches the actual transcript, including the final sentence.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Hindi TTS say a number in English?

Mixed-language context and ambiguous digit strings can influence the model's reading. Spell out the number in the intended language and compare a short sample.

Should I replace every number with Hindi words?

No. Identifiers, OTPs and phone numbers may need digit-by-digit speech; quantities and prices usually need number words. Preserve the meaning first.

Can punctuation guarantee correct pauses?

No. It can make the intended reading clearer, but listening to generated audio remains necessary.
