Hinglish AI Voice: Fix Hindi–English Switching in TTS

A script-first workflow for creators who need one natural-sounding Hindi–English narration instead of sudden accent changes or misread English terms.

18 November 2026·Vāṇī AI Editorial·How-to guide

Quick answer

For Hinglish text-to-speech, first decide how each English word should sound in a Hindi sentence. Keep the script readable, separate ambiguous abbreviations, use a consistent writing convention for repeated terms, choose a suitable Hindi or Indian-English voice/locale where supported, and listen to short test clips before generating the full narration. No spelling trick guarantees perfect pronunciation.

Key takeaways

  • Hinglish is mixed-language speech, not simply Hindi text transliterated into Latin letters.
  • Use Devanagari for Hindi passages when it improves clarity; keep brand names and technical terms consistent.
  • Distinguish a word's meaning from its intended sound before rewriting it.
  • Test voice identity, stress, pauses, and language switching on the actual output.
  • For names, dates, prices and IDs, add a separate pronunciation review.

Why Hinglish TTS changes accents

A sentence such as “Aaj hum smartphone ki battery life compare karenge” combines Hindi grammar with English product vocabulary. A speech generator has to infer which sounds, stresses and language conventions to apply. Romanized Hindi can add ambiguity: the same Latin spelling may represent several Hindi sounds, while English acronyms can be interpreted as words, letters or names.

Voice and locale settings matter, but they do not eliminate the need to review actual output. Google's Gemini speech documentation describes supported locales and configurable voice settings; it does not promise error-free code-switching in every utterance. Treat every generated clip as a draft until it has been heard.

This guide focuses on mixed-language narration. For the separate problem of digits, currency, years and OTPs, use our Hindi numbers and rupees pronunciation guide.

Before-and-after Hinglish voiceover scripts

Example 1: a tech review

Ambiguous draft: Aaj hum smartphone ki battery life aur fast charging check karenge.

Clearer narration draft: आज हम स्मार्टफोन की बैटरी लाइफ़ और फ़ास्ट चार्जिंग चेक करेंगे।

Here, the Devanagari spelling expresses a common Hindi pronunciation of borrowed English terms. It is a candidate script, not a claim that every TTS model will say it correctly. If your audience expects English pronunciation of “fast charging,” test that term in Latin script too and compare the audio.

Example 2: a creator tutorial

Ambiguous draft: Open the dashboard, phir export button click karo.

Clearer narration draft: डैशबोर्ड खोलिए। फिर “Export” बटन पर क्लिक कीजिए।

Breaking one complex mixed sentence into two short clauses makes the intended pause clearer. Keep Export in English if the video interface visibly uses that label; alternatively test “एक्सपोर्ट” for a Hindi-dominant delivery. Do not translate user-interface labels into words that viewers cannot find on screen.

Example 3: brand and acronym caution

Draft: API key paste karo aur MP3 download karo.

Test version: ए पी आई की डालिए और एम पी थ्री फ़ाइल डाउनलोड कीजिए।

Letter-by-letter rendering may help some voices, but may also sound unnatural. Compare the acronym's spoken form with the original English abbreviation, and keep whichever your intended audience recognizes. Never silently alter a brand name's official spelling in on-screen text or captions.

Example 4: a classroom explainer

Draft: Photosynthesis ka simple meaning hai plants ka food banana.

Test version: फ़ोटोसिंथेसिस का सरल अर्थ है—पौधों का अपना भोजन बनाना।

The first clause contains a scientific English loanword. Confirm its pronunciation against the terminology used in the course; the Hindi explanation should remain scientifically accurate, not merely easier to speak.

A seven-step Hinglish TTS workflow

  1. Mark the audience and register. Decide whether the narration is conversational North-Indian Hinglish, formal Hindi with occasional English terms, or English-led narration with Hindi expressions. Do not switch registers unintentionally.
  2. Underline every mixed-language term. Highlight brand names, app controls, acronyms, technical nouns, place names and proper names. These are the highest-priority listening checkpoints.
  3. Choose a script convention. Use Devanagari for Hindi text where practical; retain Latin spellings when the English pronunciation or UI label matters. Avoid randomly alternating spelling conventions for the same repeated term.
  4. Prepare a tiny pronunciation glossary. For each difficult term, store the display spelling, intended spoken reading and one acceptable alternate. This is an editorial checklist, not a claim that Gemini TTS supports a custom lexicon API.
  5. Generate a 10–20-second sample. Include at least two language switches, a difficult proper noun and a common English technical word. Listen before rendering the whole script.
  6. Fix only the failing spans. Try a different written form, a shorter clause, a clearer pause or a different voice/locale. Change one variable at a time so you know what improved the clip.
  7. Check the final stitched narration. Compare script and audio, including clipped words, abrupt accent changes and inconsistent pronunciation between chunks. Keep the original display text separate from any phonetic synthesis copy if you are also producing captions.

Gemini TTS: locale and voice settings

Google's Firebase AI Logic speech-generation documentation lists hi-IN for Hindi and en-IN for Indian English among common BCP-47 language codes. The API supports explicitly setting a language code in SpeechConfig where the selected integration and model expose that configuration. The code indicates the target locale; it is not a guarantee that every code-switched word will follow your preferred accent.

If you use Vāṇī's browser-based Hindi Studio, test the current engine's supported settings rather than assuming every backend exposes identical low-level parameters. Start with the default voice, render a short Hinglish test, then adjust only controls actually available in the interface. For expressive delivery, consult our Gemini TTS natural voice prompting guide; keep style instructions separate from words that should be spoken when the API supports structured metadata.

When working with longer scripts, use a consistent voice across segments and follow our long-text chunking workflow to review transitions. Do not assume separate generations will reproduce identical pronunciation without QA.

What to check before uploading your Hinglish voiceover

  • Language switching: Does the voice abruptly change accent or language mid-clause?
  • English loanwords: Are “dashboard,” “charging,” “update” and other recurring terms pronounced consistently?
  • Proper nouns: Does the recording say names correctly rather than relying on an automatic transcript that may normalize the error?
  • Numbers and symbols: Are prices, dates, percentages, product models and acronyms intelligible?
  • Clips and transitions: Is any word cut off at a chunk boundary?
  • Captions: Do subtitles preserve correct spelling even if the audio script used phonetic spellings?
  • Listener review: Can a fluent Hindi–English speaker understand the output without seeing the script?

Speech-to-text transcription can help identify omitted or substituted words, but it cannot always reveal a name that was pronounced incorrectly. Human listening remains important for final pronunciation approval.

Common mistakes and practical alternatives

Changing the entire script to Latin letters

Romanization can be useful, but may increase ambiguity for Hindi vowel length, retroflex consonants and proper names. Try a Devanagari-first version and compare output.

Using phonetic spelling everywhere

Phonetic approximations may improve one word and damage another. Keep phonetic substitutions limited to verified problem spans, and preserve normal spellings in subtitles and published descriptions.

Assuming a locale setting solves all switching

A locale can guide synthesis, not replace pronunciation QA. Test the same sample under Hindi and Indian-English settings only when those settings are actually available in your chosen model.

Publishing without listening to the final render

Even a good short sample cannot guarantee consistency across a full episode. Recheck after script edits, chunking and export.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI text-to-speech read Hindi and English in one sentence?

Some multilingual systems can generate mixed-language speech, but quality depends on the model, voice, text, locale and vocabulary. Test the actual sentence rather than assuming support means flawless pronunciation.

Should I type Hinglish in English letters or Devanagari?

There is no universal rule. Devanagari often makes Hindi words less ambiguous; Latin spelling may be better for exact English UI labels or brand names. Use short comparison clips to decide.

Does Gemini TTS have a guaranteed pronunciation dictionary?

Do not assume a custom lexicon is available in the Gemini API. Follow the current official API documentation for supported configuration, and use script-level rewrites and listening QA when lexicon controls are unavailable.

How do I fix English words sounding too Hindi?

Try retaining the English spelling, adding a clause break, testing a supported Indian-English locale, or switching to another suitable voice. Review whether the change makes the surrounding Hindi sound unnatural.

Will a transcript catch every mispronounced name?

No. Automatic transcription can recover the intended spelling from context even when the spoken sound is wrong. Listen to the name span, especially for people, brands and places.
