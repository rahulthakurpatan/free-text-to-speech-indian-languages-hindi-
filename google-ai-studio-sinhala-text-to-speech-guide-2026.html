Google AI Studio Sinhala Text to Speech: A Practical Voiceover Guide

If you create Sinhala videos, lessons, explainers or podcasts, you may want a voiceover without recording every line yourself. Gemini text-to-speech can generate spoken audio from text, and Google's current TTS documentation lists Sinhala among its supported languages. The important part is not simply pressing Generate: you need to prepare Sinhala text carefully, test pronunciation, listen for errors and edit the result before publishing.

Quick answer: Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS documentation lists Sinhala as a supported language. Google also identifies Google AI Studio as a place to try speech generation. Model availability and interface options can change, so check the current model selector and documentation when you use it. See the official Gemini TTS guide.

1. What Google AI Studio Sinhala TTS actually means

Text to speech (TTS) converts written text into spoken audio. In a Sinhala workflow, you provide a script written in Sinhala, select or access a TTS-capable Gemini model, direct the delivery where supported, generate the audio and review it. This is different from speech-to-text, which converts a recording into written words, and different from a live voice conversation.

Google's documentation describes Gemini TTS as text input producing audio output. It supports single-speaker and multi-speaker generation, with natural-language instructions for style, pace and tone. These controls are useful for narration, but they do not guarantee that every regional name, borrowed word or sentence will be pronounced exactly as a native speaker expects. Human review remains essential.

Google's current language table lists Sinhala for both Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS (gemini-3.8-flash-tts) and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS (gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts). The same table describes the first model as supporting 130 languages and the Lite model as supporting 101. These are model capability statements, not a promise that every voice, accent, account or interface setting will be available in every region.

2. Before generating: prepare a Sinhala script

Voice quality starts with the script. A long paragraph copied from a web page may contain awkward punctuation, mixed scripts, abbreviations and formatting that make spoken output harder to follow. Prepare the text for listening, not just for reading.

  • Use Sinhala Unicode. Keep the main narration in standard Sinhala script. Avoid mixing several fonts or legacy encodings in one passage.
  • Write shorter sentences. Break a long sentence into two or three natural spoken units. This gives the voice clearer places to pause.
  • Check names separately. Proper names, places, organisations and loanwords deserve their own short pronunciation test.
  • Handle numbers deliberately. Decide whether dates, prices, phone numbers and measurements should be spoken as words or left as numerals. Listen to a sample before producing the full script.
  • Use punctuation as direction. Full stops, commas and question marks help structure the reading. Do not insert repeated punctuation everywhere to force dramatic pauses.
  • Keep a clean source copy. Remove headings such as “Scene 1”, production notes, URLs and editor instructions unless you actually want them read aloud.

For a news explainer, for example, write the opening as a short spoken introduction rather than a dense block containing the headline, date, source links and several statistics. For a lesson, introduce one idea at a time and use transitions that sound natural when read aloud.

3. A practical Google AI Studio workflow

  1. Open Google AI Studio. Sign in with your Google account and open the current speech-generation or TTS experience. Google's Gemini Audio page links to AI Studio for trying speech generation.
  2. Check the model shown in the interface. Look for a TTS-capable model. The current Gemini API documentation lists gemini-3.8-flash-tts and gemini-3.8-flash-lite-tts. Do not assume an older tutorial's model name is still the current choice.
  3. Start with a short Sinhala sample. Use three to five sentences, including a question, a place name, a number and a sentence with a natural pause. A small test makes pronunciation problems easier to spot.
  4. Give a clear delivery instruction. Ask for clear, conversational Sinhala narration at a moderate pace. Specify the audience and purpose, such as an educational explainer or calm documentary narration.
  5. Generate and listen all the way through. Do not judge the result from the first sentence alone. Check the ending, pauses, pronunciation and consistency.
  6. Revise the script before changing everything else. If one word is misread, try rewriting that word or sentence in a clearer way. Generate a new short test and compare it.
  7. Export or save the generated audio using the controls available in the current interface. The exact buttons and supported export options may change as Google updates AI Studio.
Important: Google AI Studio's interface changes over time. If the TTS model is not visible in your account, check Google's current documentation and availability rather than relying on an old video tutorial or assuming that a model has been discontinued.

4. A reusable prompt for Sinhala narration

Use a short direction before your actual script. Keep the instructions separate from the words that should be spoken, so the model does not accidentally read the production notes aloud.

Generate clear, natural Sinhala narration for a general audience in Sri Lanka.

Delivery:
- Use a calm, conversational presenter style.
- Speak at a moderate and steady pace.
- Pronounce Sinhala words carefully.
- Use brief pauses between ideas.
- Avoid an exaggerated announcer voice.
- Keep the delivery consistent from beginning to end.

Only speak the text placed between SCRIPT START and SCRIPT END.

SCRIPT START
[Paste your Sinhala script here.]
SCRIPT END

This is a starting prompt, not a guarantee of a particular accent or pronunciation. Test it with a native Sinhala speaker. If you need a more energetic style for a short video, change only the delivery direction and compare the output using the same script.

5. How to test Sinhala pronunciation properly

A voice can sound smooth while still mispronouncing a key word. Build a small test sheet before producing a full episode. Include the words and phrases that matter to your channel: local place names, personal names, dates, currency, technical terms and words that appear repeatedly in your videos.

Do not publish a sample just because it sounds impressive to someone who does not speak Sinhala. Ask a fluent speaker to check important words, names and meaning. A pronunciation mistake in a lesson, public information video or news explainer can change the meaning and damage trust.

6. Make long voiceovers easier to edit

For a long video, generate the narration in sections such as introduction, main point one, main point two and conclusion. This makes it easier to replace one faulty passage without regenerating the whole recording. Keep the original script file and name audio clips in the same order as the sections.

  • Use one consistent delivery direction for the whole video.
  • Keep each section focused on one subject.
  • Listen for sudden changes in pace, loudness or tone at the joins.
  • Leave enough silence at the beginning and end for clean editing.
  • Use background music quietly; speech must remain easy to understand on a phone speaker.
  • Add accurate Sinhala captions and check them against the final audio.

Do not speed up every clip just to fit a fixed video length. First remove repeated sentences and unnecessary filler from the script. If you must adjust speed in an editor, listen again for unnatural syllables and clipped words.

8. Cost, access and privacy: what to verify

“Free” can mean several different things: free access to a web interface, a limited free allowance, a preview feature, or an application that asks you to supply your own API key. These are not interchangeable. Before making a production plan, check the current account terms, model availability, usage limits and any applicable billing information on Google's official pages.

Do not paste confidential scripts, unpublished client material or personal information into a third-party tool unless you understand its data handling terms. Keep API keys private. Do not put a personal key in a public video description, shared document or client-facing webpage. If you use a BYOK tool, understand where the key is stored and which service receives the request.

9. Can you publish AI Sinhala voiceovers on YouTube?

Using synthetic narration does not remove your responsibility for originality, accuracy, copyright and viewer trust. Write or properly license your script, use visuals you have permission to publish, and avoid copying another creator's narration or impersonating a real person. A generated voice is only one production component; the value of the video still depends on the research, explanation, editing and usefulness.

YouTube's current disclosure guidance focuses on realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered content that could mislead viewers, such as making a real person appear to say something they did not say or depicting a realistic event that never happened. The platform's examples distinguish these cases from ordinary production assistance. Review the current YouTube AI disclosure guidance for your particular video. When in doubt, be transparent with viewers and follow the upload flow's current disclosure questions.

Do not assume that adding an AI label automatically makes a video eligible for monetization, or that failing to label a video makes it ineligible. Disclosure and monetization are separate questions. Your channel must still meet the platform's monetization and originality requirements.

10. A simple pre-publishing checklist

  • The script is original or properly licensed.
  • The Sinhala text uses a consistent script and readable punctuation.
  • Names, numbers, dates and technical terms have been checked by a fluent speaker.
  • The entire audio has been listened to, not just a short preview.
  • The narration is clear on a phone speaker and does not compete with music.
  • Captions match what the final audio actually says.
  • Images, music and video clips are cleared for the intended use.
  • Any required AI disclosure has been completed accurately.
  • The video adds useful information rather than repeating a template with only a few words changed.

Frequently asked questions

Does Gemini TTS support Sinhala?

Yes. Google's current Gemini TTS language documentation lists Sinhala for Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS. Check the live documentation for the latest model and availability details.

Can I generate a Sinhala voice in Google AI Studio?

Google identifies AI Studio as a place to try speech generation. The exact interface and available model choices can change, so check the current TTS experience and model selector in your account.

Is Gemini Sinhala TTS unlimited and free?

Do not assume that it is unlimited. Free access, preview availability, API quotas and billing conditions can differ. Check the current Google AI Studio and Gemini API terms before planning frequent or commercial production.

How can I improve Sinhala pronunciation?

Use clean Sinhala Unicode text, shorten difficult sentences, test names and numbers separately, and ask a fluent Sinhala speaker to review the output. Change one thing at a time so you can identify what improved the result.

Can I use AI Sinhala narration for YouTube?

You can create videos using AI narration, but you remain responsible for original scripts, licensed media, accurate information and any required disclosure. Review YouTube's current policies for realistic synthetic content and monetization.
